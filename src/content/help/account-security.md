Your **Account** page (the last item in the menu) holds everything about how you sign in. It's also where [Appearance and accessibility](/help/appearance) lives.

Your archive holds years of work and people's private moments, so it's worth five minutes to make signing in safe.

## Passkeys: the easiest and safest way

A **passkey** lets you sign in with your fingerprint, face or device PIN, or with a password manager such as 1Password, instead of a password. There's nothing to type and nothing a fake website can steal.

1. Under **Passkeys**, press **Add a passkey**.
2. Your browser or password manager asks where to keep it; follow its steps.

Add one on each device you use (or one in a password manager that syncs them; the list says **synced**). For safety, adding a passkey needs a sign-in from the last 15 minutes: if it asks, sign out, sign in again, and add it.

## Two-step sign-in with an authenticator app

If you sign in with a password, add a second step: a six-digit code from an app on your phone (Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, 1Password, Authy and others).

1. Under **Two-factor sign-in**, press **Set up two-factor sign-in** and confirm your password.
2. Scan the code with your authenticator app (or type the key shown under **Can't scan?**).
3. Type the code the app shows and press **Verify and turn on**.
4. **Save the backup codes** somewhere safe, such as your password manager or a printout. Each works once if you lose your phone. They won't be shown again.

You don't need codes when you sign in with a passkey. Your administrator may require one or the other for everyone (see [People and roles](/help/people-and-roles)).

**Lost your phone and your backup codes?** Ask an administrator: on **People**, **Lost phone or passkey** turns your codes off so you can set them up again.

## Your password

At least 12 characters. A short sentence you'll remember is better than a jumble you won't. Change it under **Password**: type the current one, then the new one. After too many wrong attempts the account locks for a while, and an administrator can unlock it.

## Where you're signed in

Each browser stays signed in until it's been unused for a week. **Where you're signed in** lists them, marking **This browser**. **Sign out** any you don't recognise, then change your password.

## API keys

For scripts and other programs that work with your archive, such as an export script. A key can only do what you can, limited to the **scopes** you choose, and can expire. The key is shown **once**, when you make it: copy it then. Never share keys or put them in email.

Most people never need one. The helpers on your NAS and computers get their own keys when they pair; you don't make those here.
