Keywords (also called tags) say what's in a photo and what it's about. cf-dam keeps them as a **tree**, as Lightroom does: *Places › Norway › Bergen*, *People › Family › Anna*, *Birds › Herons*.

Keywords already in your files (from Lightroom, Photo Mechanic, Bridge and others) come in by themselves when photos are uploaded or catalogued.

## Adding keywords to photos

- On a [photo's page](/help/photo-page), type in **Tags**: matching keywords from your list appear as you type.
- In the [library](/help/library), select photos and use **Tag** to add keywords to all of them.

To add a keyword in its place in the tree, type its path with `>`: `Places > Norway > Bergen`. Any level that doesn't exist yet is made.

## How searches use the tree

A search for a keyword finds photos with it **or anything below it**: `norway` finds photos keyworded *Bergen*. **Synonyms** are found too: give *Bergen* the synonym *Bjørgvin* and a search for either finds the same photos. See [Searching](/help/search).

## The Keywords page

Choose a keyword in the tree to see and change it:

- **Name:** rename it everywhere at once.
- **Inside:** move it under another keyword (or to the top).
- **Synonyms:** other words for the same thing, separated by commas.
- **Merge into…:** combine it with another keyword, for example *Herron* into *Heron*. Every photo that had either now has the one you keep.
- Delete it (it asks you to confirm).

### When exporting

These follow Lightroom's own options, and decide what's written into downloads and XMP files:

- **Include this keyword:** untick it for keywords that only organise others, such as *Places* or *People*. Search still sees every level.
- **Include the keywords above it:** a photo keyworded *Bergen* is exported with *Norway* too.
- **Include its synonyms.**

## Colour labels

At the bottom of the page you can name the five colour labels for how you work (red for "Client choice", green for "Delivered", and so on). Keys **6** to **9** set red, yellow, green and blue in the library. The names are written into XMP, as Lightroom does.
