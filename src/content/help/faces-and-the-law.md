**This isn't legal advice.** It's what cf-dam does and the questions to ask, so you can decide with your own adviser.

## What cf-dam keeps

[Finding faces](/help/faces) keeps, for each face it finds, a box (where the face is on the photo) and a score (how sure it was). It doesn't keep facial features, face prints or anything else measured from a face, it never compares faces, and it never works out who anyone is: a person names each face. Faces are found on your own machines and never sent to anyone else for it. Clients never see them.

## Laws that may apply

Some laws protect "biometric" information about faces:

- **Illinois, BIPA** (the Biometric Information Privacy Act): written consent before collecting a "scan of face geometry", with damages per person.
- **Texas** (CUBI), **Washington** (My Health My Data) and **Colorado** have similar rules.
- **The EU and UK, GDPR Article 9**: biometric data used to identify people needs explicit consent or another legal ground, and often an impact assessment.

Whether **finding** faces, without recognising anyone, counts under these laws isn't settled. Some courts have said a scan that can't identify anyone isn't covered; at least one case about a tool that only found faces was allowed to go ahead. The GDPR treats photos as biometric data only when they're processed to identify people.

## What's up to you

You're the one taking and keeping the photos, so it's your call whether to turn face finding on, and for which work. Where the law asks for it, telling the people in your photos, or asking them, is up to you. Event work often includes other people's children and guests who aren't your clients.

If you're unsure, leave **Find faces** off, or ask your adviser first. You can pause it at any time, and **Remove all face boxes** deletes everything it found (the names you gave stay on the photos).
