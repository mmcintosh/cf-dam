# Ship containers, not plugins, to photographers' NAS

An open-source photo DAM aimed at NAS owners is viable, but only if it ships as **one canonical, unprivileged, multi-arch Docker Compose stack** and treats native vendor packages and vendor APIs as optional extras. Synology is clearly the platform to target first: about **62% of self-hosters on pre-built NAS hardware** use it, against 14% for QNAP. Photographer buying guides also pick Synology first. Self-hosting-minded photographers lean toward TrueNAS and Unraid instead. Every relevant platform runs Docker except WD My Cloud OS 5 and some low-end ARM units. Docker is also how Immich and Jellyfin already reach NAS users, while each vendor's native package format has its own signing, review and business-relationship hurdles. The harder constraints are technical. File watchers do not work reliably over SMB or NFS. Embedded databases must never sit on a network share. The typical photographer's Synology has 4–8 Zen 1 threads, 4–8 GB of RAM and **no iGPU**, so the DAM has to run *next to* the files, pull RAW previews from embedded JPEGs, and send ML and full RAW rendering to stronger machines. The competitive opening is real. Vendor photo apps and Immich are consumer gallery and phone-backup tools. No open, NAS-resident, multi-user DAM yet offers pro culling and Lightroom-compatible XMP round-tripping.

## Synology owns the photographer NAS; DIY OSes own the tinkerers

No credible analyst figure for consumer NAS vendor share exists, and the free summaries contradict each other. Global Growth Insights puts **Western Digital first at ~30%** and Synology at ~25% ([Global Growth Insights](https://www.globalgrowthinsights.com/market-reports/consumer-nas-market-105865)). That WD number almost certainly counts single-drive My Cloud "personal cloud" boxes, which cannot run third-party software. Dataintelo gives two incompatible numbers in the same report: Synology, QNAP and Asustor at 67.3%, and Synology plus QNAP at 34.8% ([Dataintelo](https://dataintelo.com/report/consumer-network-attached-storage-nas-market)). The most usable vendor split is a self-hoster survey republished in February 2026. Its provenance is unclear (no name, year or sample size). It reports **Synology 62%, QNAP 14.3%, custom builds 11.2%, Asustor 3.1%, UGREEN 2.0%, and WD plus TerraMaster 2.8% combined** ([CommandLinux](https://commandlinux.com/statistics/nas-and-file-server-os-distribution/)). Treat these figures as directional. They agree with every other signal, though.

Photographer-specific data is editorial, not survey-based, but it points the same way. Fstoppers' 2026 guide names the **Synology DS925+ best overall** and the DS1825+ best for large libraries. It picks the UGREEN DXP4800 Pro for value, TerraMaster's F4-424 and F2-425 Plus for budget and compact builds, Asustor's Flashstor 6 Gen2 for all-flash, and QNAP's TVS-h474 for ZFS integrity. It doesn't mention Unraid or TrueNAS at all ([Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/best-nas-photographers-2026)). Other 2026 affiliate roundups also lead with Synology (DS423, DS923+, DS224+), praise Synology Photos' face recognition, and cite QNAP's QuMagie for AI indexing ([findingdulcinea](https://www.findingdulcinea.com/best-nas-for-photographers-january-2026/); [requirementspc](https://requirementspc.com/best-nas-for-photographers/); [speedtesthq](https://speedtesthq.com/best/best-nas-for-photos)).

The self-hosting population looks different. In selfh.st's 2025 survey (n=4,081), **TrueNAS was used by 19.0% of respondents, Unraid by 10.1%, Synology DSM by 9.8% and OpenMediaVault by 5.0%**. 67.8% of respondents run a NAS, and **Immich was named a favorite app by 11.7%** ([selfh.st 2025 results](https://selfh.st/survey/2025-results/)). TrueNAS says its 25.04 release runs on more than 130,000 systems ([TrueNAS blog](http://www.truenas.com/blog/truenas-goldeye-25-10-beta/)). Unraid has no public user count; its 7.2 release thanked more than 5,000 beta testers ([Unraid 7.2.0 blog](https://unraid.net/blog/unraid-7-2-0)). Mainstream working photographers buy Synology. The technically minded early adopters an open-source project needs first run TrueNAS, Unraid and Docker-on-anything. Both groups matter, for different reasons.

Synology's position has a weak spot. In 2025 it locked drive compatibility on new models. Press reports say sales "dropped sharply", which Synology has not confirmed. **DSM 7.3 (Oct 8, 2025) reversed the lock for HDDs and SATA SSDs**, but M.2 cache drives still have to be on Synology's compatibility list ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/109787-synology-reverses-course-non-certified-hdds-nas-systems.html); [Guru3D](https://guru3d.com/story/synology-reverses-policy-banning-thirdparty-hdds-after-nas-sales-plummet/)). Meanwhile UGREEN, TerraMaster and Asustor sell newer Intel hardware with Docker and no drive restrictions. Lock-in is softening at the edges, which is one more reason to stay vendor-neutral.

Two dead or dying platforms create a migration pool. **Drobo, long popular with photographers, went into Chapter 7 liquidation in 2023** ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2022/07/18/storage-company-drobo-files-for-chapter-11-bankruptcy/); [512 Pixels](https://512pixels.net/2023/05/the-end-of-drobo/)). WD ended support for My Cloud OS 3 in April 2022 and guarantees **OS 5 support only "through 2026"** ([WD OS3 end-of-service](https://www.westerndigital.com/support/software/my-cloud-os/os3/os3-eos); [WD My Cloud updates](https://westerndigital.com/mycloudupdates)). These users need a way to ingest and deduplicate large trees from old SMB shares as they move to new hardware. They are not candidates for running a DAM in place. Netgear ReadyNAS is often listed among discontinued lines, but the research did not verify its end-of-life status.

## Native app stores reward business deals; Compose rewards everyone

Every commercial vendor has its own package format and gatekeeping model. On every platform that matters, Docker Compose gets around all of them.

| Platform | Native format | Trust / signing | Official listing path | Container path |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Synology DSM 7.x | SPK | Must run as non-root package user; only Synology-signed packages are "trusted", so community SPKs always show a warning | Apply, accept Developer Distribution Agreement, pass review checklist | Container Manager "Project" (Compose); SPKs can declare a Docker resource worker |
| QNAP QTS / QuTS hero | QPKG (QDK) | QNAP code signing; unsigned apps flagged or blocked by Security Counselor unless the user opts in | QNAP program (terms not found); Qnapclub community store | Container Station (QNAP's preferred route since QTS 5.0) |
| UGREEN UGOS Pro | UPK (`ugcli`) | Device-level developer authorization; packer bans privileged containers | Email template → business-team review | UPK can wrap a Compose file plus image tarballs |
| Asustor ADM | APKG | Approval via Developer's Corner; manual sideload allowed | App Central submission | Docker/Portainer apps (not researched in detail) |
| TerraMaster TOS | TPK | Direct TPK sharing allowed | Email PM team for evaluation | Docker Manager (not researched in detail) |
| WD My Cloud OS 5 | Native apps | n/a | n/a | **No supported Docker** |
| Unraid 7.x | CA XML template / `.plg` | Plugins always manually reviewed | ca.unraid.net Validate/Scan portal | Single-container templates; Compose via community plugin |
| TrueNAS CE 25.x/27 | Catalog app (Jinja Compose) | Inclusion at iX's discretion | PR to `truenas/apps` community train | Native Docker Compose, custom YAML apps |
| OMV / ZimaOS / Umbrel | `.deb` plugin / `x-casaos` Compose / `umbrel-app.yml` | Store review (Umbrel) or none (third-party stores) | omv-extras, static store, PR to umbrel-apps | Compose-native |

**Synology** is the best documented platform and the strictest. Since DSM 7.0, the publication checklist requires packages to run as a non-privileged user, raise no Security Advisor issues, cause no AppArmor denials, make no abnormal connections during install, and remove their files cleanly ([Synology Appendix C](https://help.synology.com/developer-guide/appendix/publication_review.html)). Privileges are declared in `conf/privilege` with `run-as: package`. Linux capabilities can be granted per tool ([Synology privilege config](https://help.synology.com/developer-guide/privilege/privilege_config.html)). DSM 7 also adds declarative "resource workers" for Docker, web services, MariaDB and port configuration ([Synology Developer Guide](https://help.synology.com/developer-guide/)). Official distribution means applying and accepting a **Developer Distribution Agreement** ([Synology publish guide](https://help.synology.com/developer-guide/publish_package/get_start.html)). Without that, SynoCommunity can host the package, but **"only Synology-signed packages are trusted"**, so users always see a third-party warning ([SynoCommunity FAQ](https://github.com/SynoCommunity/spksrc/wiki/Frequently-Asked-Questions)). The Syncthing package learned this the hard way when the DSM 7 beta blocked it for running as root ([mariushosting](https://mariushosting.com/3rd-party-package-support-in-dsm-7/)). The public developer guide is still versioned 7.2.2. The research found no DSM 7.3/7.4 packaging changes beyond a forum report that 7.3 "repairs" packages installed on NVMe volumes by moving them to HDD ([synology-forum.de](https://www.synology-forum.de/threads/neue-version-7-3-2-86009.140586/page-3)).

**QNAP** ties trust to its own key. QDK supports digital signatures, and later versions sign with an HSM USB token, which points to QNAP holding the key ([QNAP Wiki](https://wiki.qnap.com/wiki/QPKG_Development_Guidelines); [tailscale/qdk](https://github.com/tailscale/qdk)). Apps without QNAP's signature are flagged, and Security Counselor can stop them unless the user allows unsigned apps ([QNAP FAQ](https://www.qnap.com/en/how-to/faq/con_show.php?cid=2434)). The community Jellyfin QPKG, a wrapper around Container Station, is unsigned for exactly this reason ([kajain99/Jellyfin-QPKG](https://github.com/kajain99/Jellyfin-QPKG)). Asking photographers to turn off signature checks on a QNAP is a hard sell after the ransomware history covered below.

**UGREEN** is the newest platform and, oddly, the best fit for a container-based project. Its developer platform builds on Debian 12 with `ugcli`, and test devices need a `ugdev.sig` authorization ([UGREEN dev docs](https://developer.ugnas.com/en/doc/backend/quick-start/prepare.html)). A UPK "Docker app" is essentially a standard Compose file plus `docker save` image tarballs. The packer **rejects `privileged`, `cap_add`, host PID/IPC and the `latest` tag** ([UGREEN Docker APP docs](https://developer.ugnas.com/en/doc/backend/quick-start/develop-docker-app.html)). A clean Compose stack therefore becomes a native UGREEN app with very little extra work. Listing still goes through an email and a business-team review ([UGREEN listing flow](https://developer.ugnas.com/en/doc/flow.html)). **Asustor** (latest guide July 2023) and **TerraMaster** (guide still references TOS 5, and "cannot give direct technical support to every developer") run thinner programs, also mediated by email or review ([Asustor guide](https://downloadgb.asustor.com/developer/App_Central_Developer_Guide_4.2.2_20230712.pdf); [TerraMaster developer page](https://www.terra-master.com/us/developer)). **WD My Cloud OS 5 has no supported Docker** ([WD Community](https://community.wd.com/t/docker-support-in-os5/257063)) and should be treated purely as an SMB source.

The DIY operating systems are more open but have their own quirks. **TrueNAS Community Edition** has run apps as Docker Compose since 24.10. Third parties submit pull requests to the `community` train of `truenas/apps` with Jinja-templated Compose, a `questions.yaml` form and test values. The guidelines suggest running as the 568 apps user. Policy is strict on one point that matters for a DAM: iX excludes apps that **"interact with the TrueNAS host management layer or API"** and can reject or unpublish at its discretion ([truenas/apps CONTRIBUTIONS.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/truenas/apps/master/CONTRIBUTIONS.md)). A TrueNAS catalog app also feeds HexOS, whose first curated apps were Plex and Immich ([NASCompares](https://nascompares.com/2024/12/02/hexos-nas-software-review-of-the-beta/)). On **Unraid**, Community Applications takes XML templates through a web portal at ca.unraid.net with Validate and Scan steps, and plugins are always reviewed by hand ([CA help](https://ca.unraid.net/submit/help/repository-xml)). Compose is still not native. It comes through the community Compose Manager Plus plugin ([CA listing](https://ca.unraid.net/apps/compose-manager-plus-0wft8je0ra0zhy)). Immich's docs call that plugin the official Unraid method, because the single-container CA template relies on an unsupported community image ([Immich docs: Unraid](https://docs.immich.app/install/unraid)). Unraid's 2024 licensing change pins users who stop paying to their current minor version ([Unraid licensing FAQ](https://docs.unraid.net/unraid-os/faq/licensing-faq)). That leaves a long tail of old Docker engines: Unraid 6.12.10 ships Docker 24.0.9, which breaks Immich's healthcheck `start_interval` ([Immich docs: Unraid](https://docs.immich.app/install/unraid)). **ZimaOS/CasaOS** accept plain Compose with an `x-casaos` metadata block and third-party static stores. **Umbrel** takes `umbrel-app.yml` plus Compose via a PR review ([ZimaOS docs](https://www.zimaspace.com/docs/developer/app-store-create-from-scratch); [Umbrel community](https://community.umbrel.com/t/umbrel-0-5-2-is-here-with-community-app-stores-tor-access-toggle-search-in-app-store-and-more/9981)). On **OMV**, third-party apps run through the omv-extras Compose plugin with a dedicated non-root "appuser" ([omv-extras wiki](https://wiki.omv-extras.org/doku.php?id=omv7:docker_in_omv)).

Comparable projects confirm the pattern. Immich's Synology guide uploads a `docker-compose.yml` into Container Manager ([Immich docs: Synology](https://docs.immich.app/install/synology/)). Jellyfin's official Synology route is Docker, and a Jellyfin moderator describes the SynoCommunity package as **"just a re-wrap of our docker image"** ([Jellyfin docs](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/installation/advanced/synology); [Jellyfin forum](https://forum.jellyfin.org/t-migrate-jellyfin-package-to-docker-container-on-synology-nas)). Note that the per-platform guides for both Immich and PhotoPrism are mostly community-maintained. A project's real distribution work is keeping one Compose file stable enough that platform communities can wrap it.

## Vendor APIs are connectors, not foundations

No vendor offers a documented file-change webhook or event API. That alone rules out building a DAM's core on vendor APIs. **Synology** publishes official File Station and DSM Login Web API guides covering `SYNO.API.Auth`, list, search, upload, download, copy/move and delete ([File Station API Guide](https://cndl.synology.cn/download/Document/Software/DeveloperGuide/Package/FileStation/All/enu/Synology_File_Station_API_Guide.pdf)). The **Synology Photos API (`SYNO.Foto.*`) is undocumented**. It is known only from community reverse-engineering whose authors admit it is "by far not complete" ([zeichensatz/SynologyPhotosAPI](https://github.com/zeichensatz/SynologyPhotosAPI)). Any import of Synology Photos albums or tags will be fragile across DSM releases. The MIT-licensed `N4S4/synology-api` Python wrapper (~600 stars, 300+ APIs, self-described hobby project) can be used from any license ([N4S4/synology-api](https://github.com/N4S4/synology-api)). **QNAP's** public API PDFs date from 2013–2016 (`authLogin.cgi`, `sid`, 2-step verification in the v5.1.0 auth doc), and no current QTS 5.2 reference was found ([QTS HTTP API Authentication](https://eu1.qnap.com/dev/QTS_HTTP_API-Authentication_v5.1.0.pdf)). **Asustor, TerraMaster and WD** have no public file APIs that the research could find.

The DIY platforms have better APIs that are moving targets. **TrueNAS deprecated REST in 25.04 and removes it in TrueNAS 27** (renamed from 26 at RC.1 on Oct 6, 2026; GA targeted for early December 2026). The only API left is versioned JSON-RPC 2.0 over WebSocket, and `auth.login_with_api_key` is deprecated in favor of `auth.login_ex` ([TrueNAS 26 deprecations](https://www.truenas.com/docs/scale/26/gettingstarted/deprecations/); [TrueNAS 27 blog](https://www.truenas.com/blog/truenas-27-is-here/)). Any blog-era REST integration is now obsolete. Because the catalog bans host-API apps, snapshot or dataset integration has to be an opt-in feature where the user supplies an API key. **Unraid 7.2 builds in an open-source GraphQL API** with API keys and SSO/OIDC. It is mostly status and notifications so far, and Docker Compose support and a plugin SDK are still listed as roadmap items ([Unraid API docs](https://docs.unraid.net/API/); [Unraid API roadmap](https://docs.unraid.net/API/upcoming-features/)). **OMV** exposes only its internal `omv-rpc` layer, which third-party tools drive over SSH ([omv-mcp](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/mbgroen/omv-mcp)).

The right use of these APIs is narrow. Synology File Station and QNAP File Station can back a "pull from remote NAS" import connector. TrueNAS JSON-RPC can drive optional pre-edit snapshots. Unraid GraphQL can feed array-state awareness and notifications. Native filesystem access through bind mounts remains the primary integration.

## Network shares break watching, identity and embedded databases

### SMB3 is the only universal protocol, and the server's Samba config decides its behavior

SMB3 via Samba is the one file protocol every NAS and every desktop OS supports. The DAM cannot control the server-side settings that shape its behavior, though. Samba's `vfs_fruit` module makes macOS clients usable and must be stacked with `streams_xattr`. Its `fruit:aapl` extension sharply cuts per-file round trips during directory listing. But **`fruit:zero_file_id` (on by default) returns zero file IDs to macOS clients**, because inode-derived IDs can be recycled. Time Machine-style shares also turn off kernel oplocks and POSIX locking, which makes locks coherent only among SMB clients ([Samba vfs_fruit(8)](https://www.samba.org/samba/docs/current/man-html/vfs_fruit.8.html)). SMB3 multichannel became default in Samba 4.15 on Linux/FreeBSD, but vendors can override that default; Synology reportedly enabled it only on x86 models ([Samba WHATSNEW](https://ftp.samba.org/pub/samba/rc/4.15.0rc/samba-4.15.0rc7.WHATSNEW.txt); [blackvoid](https://www.blackvoid.club/synology-smb-4-15-9-package-brings-smb-multichannel.md)). The DAM therefore needs a "share capability probe" at setup.

NFS is a reasonable fast path for Linux hosts. S3 is not a safe baseline. **MinIO Community Edition stopped shipping binaries in October 2025, entered maintenance mode in December 2025, and was archived as "NO LONGER MAINTAINED" in February 2026** ([InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2025/12/minio-s3-api-alternatives/); [Linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/minio-ends-active-development/)). QNAP's QuObjects is the only first-party NAS S3 service found, and it no longer supports QuTS hero ([QNAP QuObjects](https://www.qnap.com/da-dk/software/quobjects)). Garage (AGPL) is a self-hosted alternative ([NixOS wiki](https://wiki.nixos.org/wiki/Garage)). S3 belongs on the list as an optional backend only. The research did not source WebDAV, SFTP or rsync characteristics. The working assumption, which is unverified, is that WebDAV and SFTP carry too much per-file overhead for scans of millions of files, and that rsync is an ingest and mirroring tool rather than a live-access protocol. AFP's removal from macOS is likewise unverified here, so AFP should not be relied on.

Throughput is sub-linear for photo workloads. In one test, copying 13.5 GB of mixed RAW, JPEG, TIFF and XMP files took **~33 s over 10GbE versus ~139 s over 1GbE, roughly 4x rather than 10x** ([PhotographyLife](https://photographylife.com/10-gbit-for-photography)). Many small sidecars dominate. As an unmeasured estimate, at 0.5–1 ms per SMB `stat`, a 2-million-file rescan costs 15–30 minutes on a LAN before reading any bytes, and hours over a 20–50 ms VPN. Scanning has to happen next to the data, and remote clients should talk to an app API, not to SMB over WireGuard.

### No shipping change-notification path works across network mounts

Client-side watching over network mounts is unreliable, and the research found no evidence of a fix landing. Linux inotify on a CIFS mount does not see changes made by other machines. Kernel work to route inotify into network filesystems was still a 2022 prototype ([lore.kernel.org](https://lore.kernel.org/all/Yhf+FemcQQToB5x+@redhat.com/T/)). The CIFS-specific `CIFS_IOC_NOTIFY_INFO` ioctl exists, but it is not inotify ([cifs ioctl log](https://sre.ring0.de/linux/log/fs/cifs/ioctl.c?showmsg=1)). **NFSv4.1 directory-delegation CB_NOTIFY is still an unmerged server patch series (v6, June 2026)**, and its author wants a complete client before merging ([LKML](https://lkml.rescloud.iu.edu/hypermail/linux/kernel/2606.1/09622.html)). SMB2 CHANGE_NOTIFY works for Windows and macOS clients, but macOS admins can disable it with `notify_off=yes` ([Dell PowerScale guide](https://infohub.delltechnologies.com/en-au/l/powerscale-onefs-macos-client-performance-and-user-experience-optimization/smb-change-notifications-5)). It also misses server-side or NFS changes unless Samba's "kernel change notify" is on, and TrueNAS maintainers warn that setting exhausts file descriptors ([TrueNAS forum](https://www.truenas.com/community/threads/truenas-13-and-kernel-change-notify-no.112880/)).

A watcher running on the NAS works but hits a hard ceiling. Immich's own watcher is "experimental", and the docs say it "likely won't work" on a network drive. Because it watches every file, it hits `ENOSPC` against the **default `max_user_watches` of 8192** ([Immich docs: External Libraries](https://docs.immich.app/features/libraries)). On Synology, raising that limit takes a root boot task in Task Scheduler, since manual changes are lost on reboot. Each watch costs about 1 KB of unswappable kernel memory. One Roon container on a Synology used up the budget and **broke DSM's own services** ([Resilio docs](https://docs.resilio.com/content/troubleshooting/error-messages/agent_run_out_of_system_notify_watchers/); [Roon community](https://community.roonlabs.com/t/synology-dsm-roon-server-ipv6-dhcpv6-broken-because-roon-exhausted-inotify-watches/322285)). Filesystem-level diffs are the efficient alternative. **`zfs diff` reports created, removed, modified and renamed files** between snapshots ([OpenZFS zfs-diff(8)](https://openzfs.github.io/openzfs-docs/man/master/8/zfs-diff.8.html)). Btrfs's `subvolume find-new` lists changed files but **does not report deletions** ([btrfs docs](https://btrfs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_sources/btrfs-subvolume.rst.txt)). Both need privileges that an unprivileged container usually lacks.

The design consequence is a layered model. Directory-level inotify (not per file), `zfs diff` or `find-new` serve as accelerators wherever they are available. A periodic scan comparing path, size, mtime and inode stays the source of truth, and it is the only reliable source of deletions. File identity cannot rest on inode or file ID across SMB, so the DAM should key on library-relative paths, use (size, mtime) as a fast stability check, and fall back to a content hash for rename reconciliation. Full hashing of 2 million 20–100 MB files means reading 40–200 TB. That only makes sense on the NAS side, with partial head-and-tail hashes first.

### Every serious vendor forbids databases on shares

The sources agree without exception. SQLite says running over a network filesystem is "at your (and your customers') peril": locks "have been known to operate incorrectly", fsync ordering is unreliable, and failures are "not frequent or repeatable." Its first recommendation is a client/server database, with **PostgreSQL "an excellent choice"**. It also advises developers to block users from choosing a remote database location ([sqlite.org](https://www.sqlite.org/useovernet.html)). Lightroom Classic refuses to launch a catalog on a network volume ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/lightroom-classic-discussions/lightroom-cannot-launch-with-this-catalog-it-is-either-on-a-network-volume-or-on-a-volume-on-which/m-p/12772940/highlight/true)). digiKam says SQLite cannot use network media and points to MariaDB ([digiKam handbook](https://docs.digikam.org/en/setup_application/database_settings.html)). Photo Mechanic Plus warns that shared catalogs "can cause the catalog database to be damaged" ([Camera Bits](https://docs.camerabits.com/en/support/solutions/articles/48001159649)). PhotoPrism recommends SSD storage for its database and cache on NAS installs ([PhotoPrism Synology docs](https://docs.photoprism.app/getting-started/nas/synology/)), and Immich wants Postgres on a local SSD ([Immich requirements](https://docs.immich.app/install/requirements/)). The DAM's database, along with any SQLite or LMDB preview cache, must be owned by one server process on local storage. Desktop clients should talk to that server over HTTP, and a desktop-only mode should check the filesystem type and refuse network paths, as Lightroom does. Desktop tools that do support multiple machines solve path differences with a root-mapping table: Photo Mechanic's "Shared Folders" and digiKam's per-client base paths ([Camera Bits](https://docs.camerabits.com/en/support/solutions/articles/48001157499)).

## The typical Synology has no GPU and 4 GB of RAM

The hardware split shapes every architectural choice. Synology's current Plus line uses two kinds of CPU. Intel Celeron J4125 models (DS224+, DS225+, DS423+, DS425+) have a UHD 600 iGPU but a 6 GB RAM ceiling. AMD Ryzen Embedded R1600/V1500B models (DS923+, DS925+, DS1522+, DS1525+, DS1825+) have no iGPU at all. Synology's acceleration APIs are also reserved for its own apps, so **GPU access from containers is effectively unavailable on most Synology models** ([hometechops](https://hometechops.com/nas/local-ai-on-nas-immich-frigate-ollama); [NASCompares DS925+](https://nascompares.com/news/synology-ds925-nas-news/)). Every j and Value model (DS124, DS223j, DS223, DS423) runs ARM on a Realtek RTD1619B with 1–2 GB of RAM. UGREEN, TerraMaster and QNAP's newer Intel boxes ship N95/N150/N5105, Pentium 8505 or Core i3-1315U chips with Quick Sync iGPUs, DDR5 and often 10GbE.

Measured ML throughput on NAS-class CPUs is scarce. The best CPU-only data point comes from a 2018 **Core i7-8559U NUC running Immich 3.3.1**, reading its library from a Synology DS723+ over NFS. It indexed **51,126 images with CLIP ViT-B-32 in about 58 minutes (14.8 images/s)** and ran face detection (buffalo_l) at 5.2 photos/s, extrapolated to about 2 hours for 40,000 photos ([mustafa.net](https://mustafa.net/2026/10/10/immich-without-gpu-intel-nuc/)). The author adds that thumbnail generation "can easily take longer" than ML. By CPU class, and not measured, a J4125 or R1600 should be roughly 3–5x slower. That puts a 50,000-photo initial index at somewhere between 4 and 15 hours, which fits the anecdotal "completes in a day" reports for a QNAP TS-464 ([needtoknowit](https://needtoknowit.com.au/blog/immich-ai-features-guide/)). Incremental ingest of a few hundred images per shoot is fine on any x86 box.

Memory is the real wall. Immich needs at least **6 GB of RAM and 2 cores (8 GB and 4 recommended)**, runs on 4 GB only with ML disabled, supports amd64 and arm64 only, and requires x86-64-v2 for its ML container ([Immich requirements](https://docs.immich.app/install/requirements/)). A French guide advises against Immich on 2 GB NAS units at all ([IT-Connect](https://www.it-connect.fr/nas-synology-comment-auto-heberger-immich-avec-docker/)). Container Manager now covers some ARMv8 models (DS220j, DS120j, DS420j, DS223, DS124), contradicting third-party guides that say otherwise ([Synology Container Manager release notes](https://www.synology.com/en-uk/releaseNote/ContainerManager)). Even so, a 1 GB RTD1619B box can host a thin agent at most. The research found no RAW-decode benchmarks on these CPUs. As an inference, a full LibRaw demosaic of 45–61 MP files takes several seconds and more than 1 GB of transient memory per worker on a J4125, while pulling the camera's embedded JPEG preview is cheap everywhere. Guides already recommend running Immich ML on a separate **N100/N150 mini-PC with at least 16 GB of RAM** rather than on a Synology ([hometechops](https://hometechops.com/nas/local-ai-on-nas-immich-frigate-ollama)). Where an iGPU exists, Intel `/dev/dri` passthrough works on Unraid, OMV and TrueNAS, provided the container user is in the render/video groups ([FileFlows](https://fileflows.com/docs/installation/unRAID/intel); [omv-extras wiki](https://wiki.omv-extras.org/doku.php?id=omv7:docker_in_omv)). TrueNAS 25.10 moved to NVIDIA's open kernel modules, which dropped several older GTX cards ([TrueNAS Goldeye blog](http://www.truenas.com/blog/truenas-goldeye-25-10-beta/)).

The practical defaults follow from this. Generate previews from embedded JPEGs first. Ship small models by default (ViT-B-32-class CLIP, lightweight face detectors). Make every job queue resumable and low-priority. Cap concurrent RAW decoding on 4–6 GB boxes. Treat **remote workers as a core feature, not an add-on**.

## Incumbents serve families; the pro catalog seat is empty

Vendor photo apps are gallery and backup layers. **Synology Photos lost server-side HEIC/HEVC thumbnailing in DSM 7.2.2.** Synology moved codec work "from the server to end devices" via its Image Assistant ([Medium/Live View](https://medium.com/live-view/heic-support-in-synology-photos-bd94a12f38e7)), and community scripts that restore it break with later DSM and iOS updates ([octavianx](https://github.com/octavianx/synology-photos-server-side-thumbnailgen); [LenweSaralonde gist](https://gist.github.com/LenweSaralonde/7e017168b0ef01682f55d852b408dac4)). Users report that Synology Photos ignores digiKam's `file.ext.xmp` sidecars ([pixls.us](https://discuss.pixls.us/t/use-of-tags-created-with-digikam-in-synology-photo/43479)). QuMagie reads `dc:subject` and flattens `lr:HierarchicalSubject` hierarchies ([DxO forum](https://forum.dxo.com/t/a-hobbyist-s-workflow/14652)). UGREEN itself says UGOS Photos "is not a replacement for Adobe Lightroom Classic" and positions it for "a finished or archived library" ([UGREEN](https://ai.ugreen.com/pages/nas-for-photo-videographer)). Vendor photo apps also carry security baggage. In September 2022 **DeadBolt ransomware exploited QNAP's Photo Station** on internet-exposed NAS units ([Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2022/09/update-now-qnap-warns-users-deadbolt-is-exploiting-photo-station-vulnerability)). That followed QNAP force-pushing firmware after Qlocker and DeadBolt earlier in 2022 ([Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/127353/malware/qnap-force-installs-update-deadbolt-ransomware.html)). A photo app is a proven attack vector, and any new DAM should ship unprivileged with no exposed admin endpoints by default, and document a reverse proxy or VPN for remote access.

Open-source servers are dominated by one project. **Immich has 116,430 GitHub stars, ships releases every 1–3 weeks (v3.3.3 on 2026-10-14), and pays full-time staff** through FUTO plus voluntary product keys ([Immich GitHub](https://github.com/immich-app/immich); [Immich FAQ](https://docs.immich.app/FAQ/)). Its design center is still phone backup and the family timeline. It writes tags to `digiKam:TagsList` rather than Lightroom's hierarchical field, caps ratings at 0–5 with no reject, and cannot edit metadata on read-only libraries because it never modifies originals ([Immich XMP docs](https://docs.immich.app/features/xmp-sidecars/); [Discussion #14454](https://github.com/immich-app/immich/discussions/14454)). Users report missing RAW+JPEG stacking and broken Fuji rendering ([Mike Lapidakis bake-off](https://empty.coffee/photo-backup-bakeoff-photoprism-vs-immich-review/)). **PhotoPrism** (40,308 stars) suits photographers better: it indexes folders read-only and stacks RAW automatically. Its open-core model keeps multi-role user management and admin security features in the paid tiers ([PhotoPrism Editions](https://www.photoprism.app/editions)). Smaller projects show how fragile this niche is. **Photoview has shipped no release since June 2024**, while LibrePhotos (MIT, 1.3.0 in October 2026) and Lychee (MIT, very frequent releases) remain active ([Photoview](https://github.com/photoview/photoview); [LibrePhotos](https://github.com/LibrePhotos/librephotos); [Lychee](https://github.com/LycheeOrg/Lychee)). digiKam and darktable are photographer-grade, but they are single-user desktop apps.

Commercial tools fill the pro tier with desktop catalogs or paid multi-user products. Lightroom Classic and Photo Mechanic Plus keep catalogs local, and Photo Mechanic Plus is explicitly "not a multi-user product." Multi-user options come from Windows-bound or subscription vendors: IMatch Anywhere ($65–$430 add-on), Daminion (a Windows server with PostgreSQL), Excire Office (~$59.90 per user per month) and Peakto Pro (€600 per two years) ([photools](https://www.photools.com/imatch-anywhere-editions/); [Daminion](https://daminion.net/docs/scheme-a-documents-are-stored-on-a-nas-or-remote-location-daminion-server-is-installed-on-a-server-pc/); [Capterra Excire](https://www.capterra.com/p/10034430/Excire-Foto-Office-Edition/); [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2025/10/08/photo-management-app-peakto-now-has-secure-online-collaboration/)). The standard community advice shows the gap. Photographers keep the Lightroom catalog and previews on local NVMe with RAW files on SMB ([raidsize.com](https://raidsize.com/blog/en/nas-for-photographers-2026)), then often run Immich or a vendor app on top for family browsing, and every layer keeps its own previews and metadata. Nothing open, browser-based and NAS-resident combines **in-place RAW indexing, fast culling from embedded previews (pick/reject, color labels, compare), Lightroom-compatible XMP round-tripping (`dc:subject`, `lr:HierarchicalSubject`, `xmp:Rating` including −1, `xmp:Label`), multi-user access and client proofing**. That combination is the open niche.

### Licensing gotchas to settle before the first release

The large self-hosted photo servers have converged on **AGPL-3.0** (Immich, Ente, Photoview, PhotoPrism Community), which blocks closed SaaS forks and, with a CLA, keeps dual licensing possible ([Immich](https://github.com/immich-app/immich); [Ente](https://github.com/ente-io/ente)). The sharpest dependency trap is face recognition. **InsightFace's code is MIT, but its pretrained models such as buffalo_l are licensed for non-commercial research only.** Immich uses them under an emailed permission that, by its own README, "does not extend to the redistribution or commercial use of their models by third parties" ([InsightFace licensing](https://www.insightface.ai/solutions/face-recognition-licensing); [Immich ML README mirror](https://ark.sudovanilla.org/Infrastructure/immich/raw/branch/main/machine-learning/README.md)). Forking Immich's ML container does not carry that permission over, so a new project needs permissively licensed face models or a commercial license. PhotoPrism's October 2026 move to ONNX models shows that this kind of re-platforming is doable ([PhotoPrism release notes](https://docs.photoprism.app/release-notes/#october-7-2026)). Codec patents are a second trap. Synology dropping server-side HEVC decoding is a warning that bundling HEVC-capable ffmpeg or libheif builds in a container shifts patent exposure onto whoever distributes it ([Synology Community](https://community.synology.com/enu/forum/1/post/189443)). Vendor terms are a third. Synology's developer documentation is proprietary, and neither its Developer Distribution Agreement nor UGREEN's Terms of Use were read in this research ([Synology Developer Guide](https://help.synology.com/developer-guide/)). Shipping an AGPL package through an official vendor store needs legal review of redistribution and source-offer duties first. Licenses for the core media libraries were **not verified** from primary sources: LibRaw, ExifTool, Exiv2, libvips, ImageMagick, ffmpeg, libheif and the CLIP/OpenCLIP weights. Background knowledge suggests Exiv2 is GPL, which would matter if the project chose MIT or Apache. Audit this before choosing the project license.

## Build a NAS-resident core with offloadable workers

**The project is viable, as long as it does not try to be a plugin.** Native packages for six vendors would mean six packaging systems, three business-mediated listing processes and two signing regimes (Synology, QNAP) that a community project cannot satisfy. The only payoff would be discoverability on boxes that already run Docker. The recommended architecture has five layers.

**DAM core.** A single OCI-image server runs co-located with the files. It owns a PostgreSQL database (or SQLite in WAL mode, accessed only by that process) on the NAS's local SSD or volume, never on a mounted share. It mounts photo shares as bind mounts, runs as an unprivileged UID (568 on TrueNAS, PUID/PGID elsewhere), and exposes an HTTP API and web UI. It should pass UGREEN's packer rules by construction: no `privileged`, no `cap_add`, no host networking, pinned tags.

**Change detection.** A periodic scan is the source of truth for every change, including deletions. Directory-level inotify is opportunistic, with an exposed watch budget and graceful degradation at the 8192 default. `zfs diff` and Btrfs `find-new` are opt-in accelerators where privileges allow. Identity uses library-relative paths plus (size, mtime) plus partial content hashes computed on the NAS.

**Work tiers.** Embedded-JPEG preview extraction and EXIF/XMP parsing always run on-box. Full RAW rendering, CLIP and face jobs go into a resumable, low-priority queue that remote workers can drain: an N100 mini-PC, a desktop GPU, or the photographer's workstation. Workers use `/dev/dri` (VA-API/OpenVINO) when present, with CUDA optional.

**Thin agent and workstation-only modes.** For ARM boxes with 1–2 GB of RAM, a thin agent handles file enumeration, hashing and embedded previews and reports to a core running elsewhere. For boxes that run no containers at all (WD My Cloud, old ARM units), the DAM runs on the workstation with a local database, scheduled SMB/NFS scans, and SMB CHANGE_NOTIFY as a hint only.

**Interop and connectors.** XMP sidecars are written in Lightroom, Capture One and Photo Mechanic-compatible fields, with optimistic-concurrency checks so the DAM never overwrites another tool's newer write. Optional connectors cover Synology and QNAP File Station import, TrueNAS JSON-RPC snapshots and Unraid GraphQL notifications, each enabled with a user-supplied key. Remote access uses plain HTTPS plus user-chosen overlays such as Tailscale or WireGuard, not vendor relays.

## Conclusion

The research turns the question around. "Which NAS should we build plugins for?" matters less than it seems, because the Docker runtime has already become the de facto NAS app platform. The vendors' own packaging trends (Synology's Docker resource worker, UGREEN's Compose-wrapping UPK, QNAP steering users to Container Station, TrueNAS rebuilding its catalog on Compose) all converge on it. What separates a successful NAS photo app is not reach but **behavior on weak, GPU-less hardware and correctness without file events**. Immich's own docs concede that its watcher and ML stack were not designed for those conditions. A DAM designed from the start around scan-as-truth, embedded-preview-first rendering and remote workers would turn the modal Synology's limits into a selling point instead of a support burden.

The differentiation is also unusually clear. Immich won consumer photo backup. PhotoPrism's photographer features sit next to a paywall for multi-user management. Commercial multi-user DAMs require Windows or a subscription. A fully open, multi-user, Lightroom-XMP-faithful culling and catalog server occupies uncontested ground. Two timing factors help it: the Drobo and WD My Cloud migration waves, and TrueNAS 27's API reset, which invalidates everyone's older integrations. Two risks need early decisions rather than later fixes. One is sustainability, given that Photoview stalled while Immich only scaled with a patron. The other is model and codec licensing: the project cannot borrow Immich's InsightFace permission and must not quietly ship HEVC decoders.
